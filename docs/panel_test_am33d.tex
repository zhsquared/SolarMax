\section{Overview}
The panel can be characterized with the AM33D multimeter alone, using two readings on
the bare panel:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Voc}: the open-circuit \textbf{voltage} (only the meter connected).
  \item \textbf{Isc}: the short-circuit \textbf{current} (meter placed across the panel).
\end{itemize}

\begin{note}
\textbf{Isc and sunlight:} a panel's short-circuit current (Isc) is proportional to the
amount of sunlight reaching it, so it varies with how squarely the panel faces the sun.
The open-circuit voltage (Voc) changes only slightly with orientation.
\end{note}

The panel is a Renogy 50\,W (RNG-50D-SS). Typical full-sun values are
\textbf{Voc\,$\approx$\,22\,V} and \textbf{Isc up to $\approx$\,3\,A}. Approximate power:
$P \approx 0.74 \times V_{oc} \times I_{sc}$.

\section{Safety and setup}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item Take readings outside in steady, strong sun (clouds change the reading).
  \item The panel does not connect to the power supply; it generates its own power from
        light. Only the panel and the meter are involved.
\end{itemize}

\begin{warn}
Shorting a solar panel is safe (unlike a battery): it can only supply its Isc, about
3\,A. Two AM33D limits apply:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,topsep=2pt]
  \item For current, use the \textbf{10\,A jack} with the \textbf{10\,A dial position}.
        The \texttt{V$\Omega$mA} jack is fused at 250\,mA, and 3\,A would blow that fuse.
  \item The datasheet limits the 10\,A range to \textbf{15 seconds at a time}. Take the
        Isc reading, then remove the probes.
\end{itemize}
\end{warn}

\section{AM33D quick settings}
The black lead stays in \textbf{COM}. Two meter setups are used:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|>{\bfseries}l|l|l|l|}
\hline
Reading & Dial position & Red lead jack & Typical display \\ \hline
Voc (voltage) & \textbf{200} in the DC-V block (V with $=$) & \texttt{V$\Omega$mA} & \texttt{21.8} (volts) \\ \hline
Isc (current) & \textbf{10A} in the DC-A block & \texttt{10A} & \texttt{2.85} (amps) \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{note}
Use the \textbf{DC} side (the ``V'' with a solid line above a dashed line, \(V\!=\)),
not the AC ``V\raisebox{0.2ex}{$\sim$}''. A leading \textbf{``$-$''} on the display means
the leads are reversed; the number is still correct. \texttt{OL} indicates an over-range
setting.
\end{note}

\section{Measurement 1: Open-circuit voltage (Voc)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Red lead in the \texttt{V$\Omega$mA} jack, black lead in \texttt{COM}.
  \item Turn the dial to \textbf{200} in the \textbf{DC voltage} block.
  \item Touch the red probe to the panel's \textbf{+} wire and black to \textbf{$-$}.
  \item Read the voltage. In full sun it is typically around \textbf{18 to 22\,V}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tip}
Press \textbf{HOLD} to freeze a reading for recording. Voc changes only slightly with
orientation.
\end{tip}

% =============================================================================
\section{Measurement 2: Short-circuit current (Isc)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Move the red lead to the \textbf{10\,A} jack (black stays in \texttt{COM}).
  \item Turn the dial to \textbf{10A} in the \textbf{DC current} block.
  \item Touch the probes across the panel's \textbf{+} and \textbf{$-$} wires. This
        briefly shorts the panel through the meter, which is acceptable for a PV panel.
  \item Read the current within a few seconds, then lift the probes. In full sun with the
        panel aimed at the sun, values reach about \textbf{3\,A}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{warn}
Keep each Isc reading under 15 seconds. After measuring current, move the red lead back
to the \texttt{V$\Omega$mA} jack, so a later probe touch does not short a circuit while
the meter is in high-current mode.
\end{warn}

\section{Procedure: tracking vs.\ not tracking}
Take both readings twice, back-to-back, in the same sunlight:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Tracking:} panel aimed straight at the sun (tracker running and pointed at
        it, or aimed by hand so its face is square to the sun). Record Voc, then Isc.
  \item \textbf{Not tracking:} panel left at a fixed, off-sun angle (for example
        flat/horizontal, or rotated about 45$^\circ$ away). Record Voc, then Isc.
  \item Repeat the pair at several times of day (morning, noon, afternoon).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Interpreting the readings}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Isc gain} $=\dfrac{I_{sc}(\text{tracking}) - I_{sc}(\text{not})}{I_{sc}(\text{not})}\times 100\%$.
        This expresses the difference in current between the two cases.
  \item \textbf{Approximate power} for any reading: $P \approx 0.74 \times V_{oc}\times I_{sc}$.
        Compute it for the tracking and not-tracking cases at each time.
  \item Voc typically changes little between the two cases, while Isc and power vary with
        orientation. Record and compare the values.
\end{itemize}

\begin{note}
For a consistent comparison, take each tracking / not-tracking pair quickly and in the
same light. If a cloud passes during a pair, repeat that pair.
\end{note}

\section{Troubleshooting}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Current reads \texttt{0} or blows a fuse:} the \texttt{V$\Omega$mA} jack
        was used for current. Use the \textbf{10\,A} jack and the \textbf{10A} dial position.
  \item \textbf{Voltage shows \texttt{OL}:} the range is too low. DC \textbf{200} is correct
        for about 22\,V. Confirm the dial is on DC, not AC.
  \item \textbf{Leading ``$-$'' sign:} the leads are reversed. Swap them, or record the
        value as shown.
  \item \textbf{Readings vary:} passing clouds, or the panel warming or partially shaded.
        Wait for steady sun.
  \item \textbf{Meter turns off by itself:} auto power-off after 15\,min. Turn it back on.
\end{itemize}
